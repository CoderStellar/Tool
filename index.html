    Methodology

    1. Scope. Five states: Alaska, California, Hawaii, Oregon and Washington. State values are aggregates over each state's area.
    2. Variables. Temperature anomaly, precipitation anomaly, wildfire risk, drought severity and sea-level rise rate.
    3. Baselines. Anomalies are expressed against a fixed reference period (1951–1980 for temperature, as in NASA GISTEMP). Other variables should state their own baseline when real data is added.
    4. Aggregation. Gridded NASA products are averaged over state polygons (area-weighted). Sea-level rise applies to coastal tide-gauge or altimetry cells only.
    5. Normalization. The risk profile chart rescales each variable to 0–100 across the five states so they can be compared on one axis. A high score means higher relative to these five states, not high in absolute terms.
    6. Pipeline. Download → clip to state boundaries → aggregate → export to data.js. The site itself is static and does no processing.

    Guardrails

    • Placeholder data. Every number in this prototype is illustrative. Charts carry a "sample" tag until real data replaces it.
    • Not a forecast. This tool describes conditions and past trends. It does not predict future events or give emergency, insurance, planning or legal advice.
    • Resolution limits. State averages hide large local variation (for example Alaska's interior vs. coast, or California's deserts vs. coast).
    • Uncertainty. Satellite and reanalysis products have errors, gaps and revisions. Real data should ship with uncertainty ranges where available.
    • Relative scores. Rankings compare these five states only and say nothing about other regions.
    • Attribution. Do not imply NASA endorsement. Credit every dataset in Sources.
    • Privacy. The site collects no personal data and has no accounts or analytics.
    • Accessibility. Keyboard-navigable tabs, labelled controls and text alternatives (ranking list, detail panel) for charts and map.
